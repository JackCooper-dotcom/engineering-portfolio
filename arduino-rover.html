Group Project Robotics

Arduino Rover

An Arduino-controlled vehicle designed to pick up a block, weigh it and navigate an obstacle course.

Completed rover

Objective

The task

The rover needed to autonomously locate, pick up, weigh, and determine the material of the block. The rover had to print either light, medium, or heavy, along with foam or plastic, to an external display screen. After picking up the block, the vehicle had to autonomously navigate an obstacle course and finally go over or under the finish line. Additionally, the design of the rover was subject to strict mass and price limits, along with restrictions about where certain parts could be purchased from.

Map of Arena

System Design

How the system was structured

The rover combined a mobile base with a linear actuator for the pickup mechanism, driven by motors and monitored with sensors, all controlled through an Arduino Uno. We also utilized a wifi module to connect to an overhead camera which scanned an Aruco marker that was taped on the top of our rover to track its movement.

CONTROLLER
Arduino Uno
DRIVE
Motors, wheels (radius ≈ 3 cm)
PICKUP MECHANISM
Linear actuator, ramp
TARGET MASS RANGE
≈ 100–300 g

Mechanical Design

Mechanical components

The mechanical aspects of the rover included the wheels, chassis, ramp, and the linear actuator mechanism used to pick up the block. Designing this system meant accounting for concepts like torque, RPM, friction, and force to make sure the motors and mechanism could actually move and lift the block reliably while also being mobile enough to navigate the course.

Individual Contribution: I assembled and created the parts needed for the chassis and ramp part of the rover. I modeled the base of the vehicle and laser cut it out of recycled PLA. I also 3D printed the walls and attached them to the base with glue and screws.

Electronics

Sensors & wiring

The rover used sensors alongside the Arduino Uno and motors to support the pickup, weighing, and material determining tasks. We heavily relied on ultrasonic sensors for most of the rover tasks. We used them to determine the object's material by using the fact that the foam block absorbed more sound waves than the plastic. This difference was calculated and written into the code for the Arduino to print out which material it was seeing based on the reading from the ultrasonic sensor. Additionally, the ultrasonic sensors were used in general navigation code to get around the obstacles. We also used one piezo sensor on the bottom of the ramp to weigh the mass of the block. Lastly, linear actuators were used to move arms that would hold the block in place so we could get a consistent reading of its mass and material. All of these components were wired and coded directly through the Arduino.

Individual Contribution: I worked mostly on the ultrasonic sensors. I spent most of my time figuring out and coding the difference in readings between the plastic and foam blocks. I also worked on wiring the ultrasonic sensors into the Arduino and making sure they were mounted correctly on the vehicle to get accurate readings.

Testing

Putting it through its paces

We tested the rover numerous times and the changes can be seen through how the CAD models changed over time. One of our first major changes was with the 3D printed arms that held the block in place. When testing with our original arms, we found that the block was not close enough to the ultrasonic sensors and the arms did not consistently get the block in the same place. We then changed the design of the arms to fix these problems by offsetting their heights and lengths. We also constantly tested our obstacle navigation code on the actual arena and tweaked it after every test run. The hours of testing each component allowed us to minimize the errors on the rover.

Problems & Iterations

What went wrong, and what changed

  • One issue we faced was with our Arduino overheating. The day before the final run-through, our Arduino burnt out and we didn't know why. Eventually, we figured out that our kill switch was poorly wired, causing a constant flow of current into the Arduino. After solving this issue, we had very little time to further test our final code. However, despite this major issue, we still had several successful run-throughs of the course.
  • The chassis was the most iterated part of the rover. We went through four different attempts at a design before we came up with the final model. Our first idea was to use laser-cut acrylic for the chassis because it was strong and easy to put together. The issue was that our rover became too heavy and our motors couldn't fit through the precut holes. We then decided to use a fully 3D printed model to make it light while still having internal strength. However, the issue we ran into was that the provided 3D printers couldn't print the long, flat bottom without warping it in some way. All of the prints we made were unusable due to this. So, we shifted to the final design of connecting a laser-cut PLA bottom with 3D printed side walls. This strategy worked because the chassis relied less on 3D printing and contained the optimal strength while also not being too heavy.
  • The motors we decided to buy were not perfect and caused us to use different strategies to accommodate. The issue with our motors was that they were too strong and had too much torque for what our vehicle needed. As a result, we utilized pulse-width modulation in our code to monitor the output torque to what we needed it to be. This strategy worked very well and saved us from having to buy new motors.

Final Result

How it performed

During the final runs, our rover was able to complete 5/6 mission tasks cumulatively. We ultimately had the second highest score in our class section. Additionally, our rover was nominated for the course-wide "Best Constructed" award, which went to the vehicle that had the best and most creative and organized construction.

Lessons Learned

Takeaways

This project was a good introduction to how mechanical design, electronics, and code all have to work together. What might look fine on paper still has to deal with real friction, real motor limits, and real wiring. I also got experience with what it's like to be a part of an engineering team working on a real project. I learned what it's like to delegate tasks along with completing my own by a strict deadline. Overall, this project was my first introduction to the engineering world and taught me a lot about how real engineers problem-solve and work together to achieve their goals.
